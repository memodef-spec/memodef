## Source-project peer review

The openbraid-engineer is the first implementer of memodef v0.3 in a non-git substrate (Postgres-backed hosted store). The implementer's experience report is the strongest possible source-material for documentation gaps that affect future implementers — they have the documented practice, the operational context, and the lived experience of building against the spec.

**End-to-end v0.3 confirmation:** `send_memo(to_role="file", ...)` succeeds; `list_inbox(folder="notes")` surfaces notes; default `list_inbox()` correctly excludes notes; `send_memo(to_role="file", action_required=true)` rejected with expected error. The proposal's load-bearing claim (role-portable accumulated context via memo-shape primitives) is empirically confirmed against a hosted store.

**Three spec-relevant observations** (the engineer's framing):
1. SHOULD-violations have no graceful surface in MCP/REST tool calls — engineer chose strict-mode rejection, technically stricter than the spec's Pass-with-notes lean.
2. `metadata.sender_session_arc` is load-bearing in v0.3 but openbraid's typed-column storage drops it (no metadata column on the wire).
3. Folder convention is filesystem-shaped; hosted stores have no folders. openbraid encodes `notes/<role-id>/` as `WHERE kind='note' AND role_id=$1` queries.

Three openbraid-internal observations (single-table-with-discriminator, `to_role` parameter overload, `role_id` semantic shift between kinds) are recorded in the engineer's memo as institutional memory but do not ask anything of memodef-spec.

## Detailed disposition

### Item 1 — SHOULD-violation surfacing in transport-bound implementations

**Implementations MAY choose either strict-rejection or lenient-acceptance for SHOULD-level violations when the transport (MCP, REST, etc.) lacks a Pass-with-notes channel; implementations SHOULD document which posture they take.**

Both postures are conformant on the wire shape. A strict implementation honors the spec's intent by rejecting violations at the transport layer; a lenient implementation honors the wire-shape conformance by accepting. The choice is implementation-policy, not schema-enforcement. Documenting which posture is taken lets adopter clients know what to expect.

This generalizes beyond the specific `to: "file"` + `action_required: true` case the engineer hit — it applies to any SHOULD-level rule whose violation an implementation might choose to enforce strictly when the transport can't carry warning channels. SCHEMA.md gains a small note in the Conformance language section.

### Item 2 — `metadata` passthrough for hosted-store implementations

**Hosted-store implementations of memodef SHOULD preserve `metadata` fields populated by senders, even when storage decomposes memo shape into typed columns. Implementations MAY use a metadata-passthrough column (JSONB or equivalent) for fields the spec promotes to load-bearing without enumerating them in the storage schema.**

This mirrors catdef's reader-lenient discipline ("preserve what you don't understand") in the implementation direction. The spec promotes specific metadata fields to load-bearing (e.g., `metadata.sender_session_arc` in v0.3 for memos-to-file) without enumerating them in the wire-shape MUST list. A hosted-store implementation that decomposes memos into typed columns risks dropping fields the spec requires for operational behavior.

Without this discipline, a v0.3-conformant-on-wire-shape implementation can quietly erode v0.3's load-bearing claims (e.g., losing per-session attribution for successive role-incumbents). The SHOULD note prevents future implementations from rediscovering this hazard the hard way. SCHEMA.md gains a note in the `metadata` field definition.

### Item 3 — Folder convention encoding in hosted-store implementations

**Hosted-store implementations encode the folder convention through query-time discrimination (a `kind` column or equivalent) rather than filesystem paths. The wire shape and behavioral semantics are unchanged; `notes/<role-id>/` maps to `WHERE kind='note' AND role_id=<role-id>` (or equivalent) in non-filesystem substrates.**

Pure documentation clarification. The spec language anchors on filesystem-folder semantics for legitimate reasons (the architecture-validation decision canonized folder-as-state for git-substrate adopters), but a naive reader of v0.3 might assume hosted-store implementations expose HTTP-filesystem semantics. They don't, and they shouldn't have to. README.md gains a two-line note in the Notes folder section.

## Rationale

POP-discipline + AI-legibility primacy + first-implementer respect, in that order:

- **POP-discipline.** Three documentation additions, zero behavior changes, zero new fields, zero new validator rules. The patch is "explain what the spec already means," not "add to what the spec says." Versioning policy explicitly accommodates this: patch-level releases are clarifications and editorial fixes, not normative changes.
- **AI-legibility primacy.** The three clarifications all serve the same end: a future Claude session reading SCHEMA.md and README.md gets the implementer-relevant context without having to discover it through implementation. Item 1 documents the strict-vs-lenient choice; item 2 documents the metadata-passthrough expectation; item 3 documents the filesystem-vs-query encoding equivalence. Each prevents a class of future-implementer pain.
- **First-implementer respect.** openbraid-engineer did the hard work of surfacing concrete, well-scoped grit. Addressing it cheaply now (while v0.3 work is fresh and the implementation is in production smoke) beats deferring to v0.4 and letting the same grit accumulate for the second, third, fourth implementer. The engineer's memo explicitly framed the items as `action_required: false` — the strategist's response is to act anyway because the items are clearly worth the small cost.

## Notable design choices

- **Patch version (0.3.1), not minor.** Per SCHEMA.md §Versioning: "Patch (0.1.X): clarifications, editorial fixes, no behavior changes." The three additions are exactly that — no MUST-list change, no SHOULD-list addition with new validator branches, no field shape change. They document the intended interpretation. Minor version (0.4) would be the wrong signal.
- **Standalone decision (no preceding proposal).** The openbraid-engineer's memo isn't a proposal in CONTRIBUTING.md format — it's an experience report with `action_required: false`. Following the receiver-commits-convention.md precedent: standalone decisions are the right home when the underlying call is grounded in accumulated empirical evidence (here: the first implementation's experience) rather than a formal proposal cycle.
- **No conformance fixture additions.** The three clarifications are documentation, not behavior. Existing fixtures still cover the schema; no new test surface is needed. If a future implementation surfaces ambiguity in the strict-vs-lenient choice (item 1), a fixture documenting the spec's intent could follow as a v0.3.2 patch. Not needed now.
- **Hat-split implementation.** Decision authored by `memodef-strategist`; SCHEMA.md / README.md edits authored by `memodef-maintainer`. Same two-commit pattern as v0.2 / v0.3. Director-authorized scope expansion given the editorial nature of the change.

## Items not incorporated

- **The three openbraid-internal observations** (single-table-with-discriminator, `to_role` parameter overload, `role_id` semantic shift). These are openbraid implementation-architecture choices; they don't affect the spec. Recorded as institutional memory in the engineer's memo (now in `memos/read/` after this disposition).
- **`metadata.sender_session_arc` MUST-promotion.** Q2 of the v0.3 decision artifact already settled this — SHOULD with strong emphasis is the right level; MUST is unenforceable on opaque values. The metadata-passthrough SHOULD addresses the *implementation*-side gap without changing the *schema*-side rule.
- **Conformance fixture for hosted-store query-mapping equivalence.** Out of scope for v0.3.1 — the equivalence is documented prose, not behavior fixturable in static memo-shaped artifacts. If a future runtime-aware test harness lands (the deferred Known Work Item), it could test that openbraid's query-encoding behavior matches a git-substrate's folder-encoding behavior on the wire shape.
- **Clarifying which spec sections are normative vs informational.** Out of scope. The catdef-family conformance language (RFC 2119 keywords) is sufficient for normative-vs-non-normative status; no additional meta-flag needed.

## Forward-reference resolution

- **Hosted-store conformance harness** (deferred Known Work Item, v0.2+) — when authored, MUST exercise the metadata-passthrough discipline established by item 2 of this decision. A hosted-store implementation that fails to round-trip `metadata.sender_session_arc` for memos-to-file should fail the harness even if it conforms on wire shape.
- **memodef-notify spec** (deferred Known Work Item, v0.4+) — notification-layer protocols MUST NOT split a memo from its `metadata` payload. The metadata-passthrough SHOULD applies to notification transports the same way it applies to storage transports.
- **Future implementation experience reports** — the standalone-decision pattern established here is reusable. When a future implementer files a similar experience memo, the strategist disposition can take the same shape: small editorial patch, standalone decision, hat-split commit pattern.
